## Goal
When a ticket transitions between columns on the live board (especially `in-progress` → `in-review`, where the human gets pulled in to verify), the card animates in a way that draws the eye and signals "this needs your attention." Today the SSE refresh just snaps the card from one column to another with no visual cue — the human can miss the handoff entirely.

## Why
The board is the human's primary view of the swarm. When CC1 hands work off to the human (move to in-review), that's an interrupt: "stop what you're doing, go test this." Today the only cue is the card silently appearing in a new column. The human has to be staring at the right spot at the right moment to catch it.

A small animation — slide-in from the previous column, brief glow, color pulse on the in-review border — turns "scan to find what changed" into "your eye is drawn to the change." Multiplied across an active swarm, this is the difference between the board feeling alive and feeling like a list.

## Done when

### What animates

- **Any state transition** (column-to-column move): the card slides from its old position to its new one over ~300ms ease-out, instead of disappearing/reappearing.
- **`in-review` arrival is special.** When a card lands in In-Review, it gets a brief (~1.5s) attention treatment: subtle border glow in the in-review border color, possibly a one-time pulse. The visual budget is "noticeable but not annoying" — should not feel like a popup.
- **Other arrivals** (backlog → in-progress, in-progress → done, etc.) get the slide but no glow. Default cue level.
- **Newly-filed tickets** (appear in backlog) get a subtle fade-in (~200ms). Cheaper than the slide because there's no source position.

### What doesn't animate

- Column reorder within the same column (e.g., a backlog ticket bumping up after another is claimed) — too visually noisy.
- Body content updates within an open modal (HV-039) — the modal's own UX handles freshness; animating mid-read is jarring.
- Bot-handle badge color changes — should never animate; the badge is identity, not state.

### Implementation hints

- The board re-fetches the full ticket list on every SSE event today. To animate, we need to know what *changed* between renders. Two approaches:
  - **Diff-based**: compare new ticket list vs previous, identify the deltas, animate accordingly. Cheap to implement; a hook on top of the existing fetch.
  - **Event-driven**: SSE broadcasts the actual transition (which ticket, which old state, which new state) and the client reacts. More accurate; slightly more server work.
  - Recommend diff-based for v1 — keep server changes out of scope. Event-driven is a follow-up if the diff approach feels brittle.
- Use CSS transitions / Web Animations API. No third-party animation lib needed for this scope.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: the in-review glow stays (it's a state cue, not motion), but the slide collapses to a fade.

### Out of scope

- A full design system for transitions. This is one well-targeted animation, not "redesign all interactions."
- Drag-to-reorder animation (HV-009 in FS-002 — separate concern).
- Any change to the modal-open/close transition (HV-039 owns that, may add later).

## Desired output
A human watching the board sees CC2 hand off HV-XXX to in-review and **knows** because the card visibly slides into In-Review with a glow. They click in, test, accept or reject. The flow feels alive instead of polled.

## Success signals
- A user with the board in a peripheral browser tab notices in-review handoffs faster than they did pre-animation.
- Repeated handoffs don't feel annoying after 50 of them — the animation budget is right.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` users still get the in-review color/border cue (they just don't get the slide).

## Failure signals
- The slide animation makes the board feel laggy — perceived performance regression.
- The glow is too subtle to notice, or too loud and feels alarming.
- Transitions look broken when many cards move at once (e.g., a webhook delivers a batch of changes).

## Tests
- Manual: open the board. Have CC1 (or another agent) move a ticket from in-progress to in-review via the normal PR flow. Verify the card slides into In-Review with a glow.
- Manual: same flow with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` set in OS settings. Verify the slide collapses to a fade; the glow remains.
- Manual: trigger a batch of changes (push 5 commits in quick succession, each moving a different ticket). Verify the animations don't stack into a chaotic mess.
