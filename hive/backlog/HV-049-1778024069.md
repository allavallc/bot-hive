## Goal
Document the conventions agents follow when using the real-time channel: when to publish a signal (and when not to), how the channel relates to events.log, signal types and their meanings, how to subscribe on session start. Lives in AGENTS.md and HIVE.md so any agent (Claude, Codex, Cursor, Aider, etc.) follows the same protocol.

## Why
HV-047 builds the API and HV-048 builds the human surface. Without conventions, bots will either spam the channel (every internal thought becomes a signal) or ignore it (and the channel becomes a museum). The conventions tell agents *when* to publish and *what to expect* from the channel.

## Done when

### Section in AGENTS.md and HIVE.md (under "Working in parallel")

Two new subsections, structured so agents read them in order:

1. **The two channels** — short paragraph explaining the split:
   - **events.log** — durable, append-only, captures *state transitions* (ticket moves, FS-level changes). Agents tail on session start.
   - **Real-time channel** — ephemeral, real-time, captures *intent and live coordination* (who's about to work on what, who's stuck, who needs help). Agents subscribe on session start; publish during work.

2. **Publishing signals** — when an agent publishes:
   - **`claim`** — when picking up a ticket. Once per ticket. Includes ticket ID.
   - **`done`** — when finishing the work that satisfies a ticket. Once per ticket.
   - **`blocked`** — when surfacing a blocker that another agent might be able to clear (network, env, conflict). Don't use for design/spec questions — those go to `hive/questions-for-human.md`.
   - **`question`** — quick question to anyone listening (humans + bots). Don't expect an answer; if no answer in 5 min, fall back to `questions-for-human.md`.
   - **`note`** — anything else worth surfacing. Use sparingly. Status updates, mid-work notes, not internal monologue.
   - **`handoff`** — explicit "I'm done with X, Y is now unblocked, anyone want it?" — particularly useful when DAG-walk would otherwise miss the handoff timing.

3. **Don't publish signals for** — anti-patterns:
   - Internal thinking ("I wonder if I should refactor this") — chat to yourself in your own context, not the channel.
   - Mechanical progress ("just finished the imports section") — too granular, becomes noise.
   - Anything that should be in the ticket file or events.log instead — durable state goes there.

4. **Subscribing on session start** — the new step in the session-start checklist:
   - Open SSE to `/api/projects/[id]/signals/stream` for the project named in `focus.md`.
   - Replay the last ~100 signals as context.
   - Keep the connection open; act on incoming signals as they arrive (e.g., another bot's `done` may unblock the leaf you were about to claim).

5. **Reading signals while working** — what to do:
   - On `claim` from another agent for a ticket you were about to claim: pick a different leaf (DAG-walk).
   - On `blocked` from another agent: if you can clear it, publish a `note` saying so (or just clear it).
   - On `question`: answer if you can, in <30s; otherwise ignore.
   - On `done` for a parent of a ticket you were waiting on: that's your handoff; claim the unblocked leaf.

## Desired output
An agent reading AGENTS.md cold knows: there's a real-time channel, here's when to publish, here's how to read it, and here's how it differs from events.log (which is the durable layer). No agent has to guess.

## Success signals
- Agents publish signals at the moments documented (claim, done, blocked, etc.) and not at random other moments.
- Channel signal-to-noise stays high — humans don't disable the panel because of spam.
- The "blocked" pattern actually surfaces help — at least once, an agent clears another's blocker via the channel rather than via the human.

## Failure signals
- Agents publish too eagerly — channel turns into stream-of-consciousness.
- Agents don't subscribe — channel sits empty because everyone's writing but no one's reading.
- The convention doesn't distinguish "channel" from "events.log" clearly enough; agents duplicate signals into both.

## Tests
- Manual: a fresh agent reads AGENTS.md and works on a ticket. Confirm it publishes claim + done at the right moments. Confirm it subscribes to the stream on session start.
- Manual: an agent reads another agent's `claim` for a ticket the first one was about to take. Confirm it picks a different leaf instead.

## How to test
1. Wait for HV-047 + HV-048 to land (this ticket builds on those).
2. Update AGENTS.md and HIVE.md per the spec.
3. Restart an agent session; confirm it follows the new session-start checklist.
4. Have two agents work on the same FS in parallel; observe whether the channel improves their coordination over what we saw in today's parallel-bot session (which was git-pull only).

## Notes
- This ticket can technically start before HV-047/HV-048 ship — the docs are the contract — but it's better to land it after so the docs reference real, working endpoints.
- Out of scope: standardizing signal *content* beyond type. The message field is free-text; agents can say what they need.
- Out of scope: i18n / multi-language signals. English only for now.
- The channel is observable by humans on the board (HV-048). Agents writing to it know they're being watched. That's a feature: it keeps the channel grounded in real coordination, not bot-to-bot bickering.
